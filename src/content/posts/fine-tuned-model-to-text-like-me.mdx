here's the base model vs the fine-tune, same prompt, no system prompt, no examples:

| prompt | base (untuned) | fine-tuned |
|---|---|---|
| "abe kaha tha kal raat" | "Arre wah! Kal raat ka scene yaad hai? 😂🤣 Chuttiye, tu toh..." | "sone de bkl" |
| "kya kar rahi ho" (romantic) | "Hey! Just thinking about you and how much I miss your voice 😏✨" | "paise kamane 💀" |

then i gave the BASE model 4 of my actual messages as in-context examples. state-of-the-art few-shot. still lost to the fine-tune that got zero examples — reply-only perplexity 30.6 vs 14.9.

a smarter base is a better generic chatbot. the fine-tune is the opposite: it collapses all that capability into one specific terse shape. you can't get there with prompting. a prompt is a costume. training changes the body.

## why cloning voice is harder than it looks

the hard part isn't casual. casual is easy — just say "reply briefly, you're a GenZ indian dude." the hard part is **register switching**. i text differently to everyone. close friends: clipped, crude, assumes shared context. mum: full sentences, respectful. younger cousins: sassy but clean. romantic: warm, low-key, not thirsty. one model has to hold all of these, switch on context, and not bleed them together.

you can't prompt-engineer that. you can tell a base model "be respectful now, this is your mum" and it'll approximate. but approximation isn't the thing. the thing is the trained default — before you've even named the context, the model already knows the register from how the conversation started.

## 73k messages, a few decisions that mattered

instagram DM export. 109 threads, 29k messages of mine. a few non-obvious calls:

**6-hour gap segmentation.** split on 6h idle periods to get sessions, not infinite scrollback. the model needs to learn how to open conversations, not just react in the middle of one.

**train on responses only.** loss-masked to only my messages. the model learns to generate me, not the person texting me.

**multi-turn, not sliding windows.** early instinct was to chunk. wrong — multi-turn is how register is learned. the model sees the whole exchange and then predicts my reply. without that context it can't know who it's talking to.

**reels as context.** a reply to a meme without the meme is meaningless. i kept reel replies with `[reel context: description]` prefixed. some of the tightest training data in the set — one meme, two words, perfect.

## the T4 saga

free kaggle GPU. Qwen3's hybrid attention requires bf16. T4 doesn't have bf16. NaN'd immediately on fp16. burned three attempts. confirmed a known unsloth bug. switched to Lightning AI's free L4 — 60 minutes, 298 steps, 2 epochs, LoRA r=32 (42.5M/4.58B params = 0.93%). then reran on 9B with an L40S in 35 minutes. 9B coherence is visibly better — holds 6-turn threads, stops hallucinating cricket scores.

stack: Lightning (train) → HF Hub (weights) → Modal scale-to-zero (serve) → Netlify (frontend). ₹0 out of pocket.

## the cuss problem

data is friend-heavy, so the model defaulted to cussing across registers. elder_fam (mum, aunts) leak was 2% — not bad, but 2% of the time the model would drop a gaali in a message to a family member. unacceptable.

DPO fixed it to 0%. but it over-generalized — taxed close_friend crudeness too, 8% → 3%. alignment tax. you trade one thing for another.

**decision-D:** instead of one compromised model, serve two adapters on one base. SFT adapter for close friends (crude, authentic). DPO adapter for clean registers (family, professional). hot-swap per register at inference time. 0% leak in clean contexts AND intact crude voice in crude contexts. the architecture is the compromise, not the weights.

## every place the model was wrong and i caught it

the AI built the pipeline. the judgment was mine. the correction list:

- `chod koi game khel` — cuss-detector flagged it. "chod" = "chhodna" = *leave* in hinglish romanization. caught a false positive that would've shipped.
- `pakistan jeet gya` — model invented a cricket score. completely out of character. became the reason i added RAG-for-facts: persona fine-tunes don't know current events, they know your voice.
- the fp16/bf16 dtype catch before committing to kaggle training.
- `"is it the weights or just the prompt?"` — asked this twice, drove the base-vs-tuned comparison. without that skepticism there's no defensible claim.
- first frontend UI looked like every claude-generated chat interface. redirected it.

the AI ran the pipeline. the human made it good. that's [the actual job](./ai-floor-ceiling) — and it's more specific than it sounds.
