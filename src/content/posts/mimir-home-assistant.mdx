the main reason i wanted to make Mimir is my habit of doomscrolling. now everyone doomscrolls, but i noticed that my feed across social media was just wars, fights, AI ending all life as we know it and whatnot. all of which might just come true, but i ain't gonna stop any of that — so i didn't want to worry about stuff i can't control. i barely control the controllables, bro, this was too much for me. 2nd reason was the childhood fantasy of having a Jarvis-like system, obviously.

## what it is

a personal assistant that blocks my instagram, my twitter (not gonna call it X), and closes my steam executable so that i can't keep looking at a screen instead of focusing on the task at hand.

is it effective? YES. my screentime has gone down, i've started reading again, no more cortisol spikes whenever anthropic tweets something. life's good.

## how it's wired

it's a Hermes agent running on my PC via WSL2 that connects to my phone via a custom flutter app. why Flutter, you'd ask? easy to vibe-code and allows Hermes to control my phone end to end.

funny thing is i was building my other app (Accountify — a Flutter app for tracking personal accountability) and was trying to get Claude to test the app end to end, and an idea struck — why not ask Claude to use wireless ADB debugging to simulate what a user would do? gave it a screenshot-to-chat tool and voilà, it found visual bugs on top of testing the whole flow end to end while i watched in awe.

but how do you get an LLM to securely connect to your phone all the time and not expose all the data to some public port? the answer is a VPN like Tailscale — it connects my PC, WSL, and phone to one private network.

one catch: WSL2 mirrored networking is broken on my Windows build, so WSL can't bind directly to the Tailscale interface. fix is `mimir_wsl_proxy.py` — a python script running on Windows that just forwards traffic from the Tailscale IP  and LAN IP  into WSL  where the actual companion lives.

and how does Mimir interact with the phone? via an open-source tool called Shizuku. so when i ask Mimir to send a message to my friend, it finds WhatsApp, opens it, searches for the friend, takes a screenshot, clicks on them, types the message, and sends it.

crazy right? nope — it uses 90 tool calls and $2 worth of DeepSeek tokens, which are dirt cheap, but still wasted so much.

## the downsides

the battery drain is crazy. sometimes the watch timer doesn't count accurately or updates a few minutes late, so if i intended a 15-min break i can get like 20-22 mins — this is a known bug where `_currentApp` never clears via the usage-access fallback, so the watcher overcounts. honestly the token usage is wild for new tasks, and to optimise it i have to build a specific tool for the task or set of tasks — at which point i'm like, is this the best way to achieve what i set out to achieve?

and the sad answer is no, not at all. the easiest and most efficient solution i found was giving my phone to my mom from 6:30 pm to 10 am. i'm WFH from 10 to 6, so yeah — out of sight out of mind beats harness engineering here.
## why "Mimir"?

i just love God of War. the whole mainline saga is crazy, banger after banger, but i especially love the last 2 entries. what better name to give an all-knowing assistant than the smartest man alive?

seems fitting. i just ask Mimir to limit Insta usage to 2 hours and it keeps track and actually blocks the whole screen when 2hrs are up — none of that "add 15 mins" bs that Insta gives.
